Reading the map

Every rectangle is a folder or file, and its area is the space it takes. Folders draw their contents inside themselves, so you can see where space goes several levels down at once.

Tiles

  • Area is size. The biggest folder on screen is the biggest rectangle.
  • Folders inside folders. A folder big enough to hold its contents gets a title strip with its name and size, and its children are drawn inside it.
  • Tint is the kind: apps, games, code, cache, media, documents or system. It's guessed from folder and file names, so it's a hint, not a verdict. A folder that matches nothing takes its parent's kind, so a game's folder stays “Games” all the way down.
  • Depth of colour is size. Bigger tiles are darker in the light theme and lighter in the dark one, on a log scale from 100 MB up to the folder on screen.
  • Hatching marks what you can reclaim. Dense diagonal lines mean a cleanup rule marks it Safe to clean; sparse lines mean Your call. See Cleanup rules.
  • File names are in italics; folder names are not.

Small items

To keep the map readable, anything under 100 MB is folded into one grey tile per folder, labelled N small items. Point at it to see how much space they add up to. They're still counted in every total.

The side panel

The panel describes whatever is under the pointer, or the folder on screen when the pointer is elsewhere. Press Esc to go back to the folder.

  • Name, path and size, with a bar showing its share of the folder it's in.
  • Of …: the same share as a percentage. Files: how many files it holds. Kind and Cleanup: its tint and its verdict, if any.
  • Open zooms into a folder, and Show in Explorer opens its location.

Below that is the cleanup list, grouped into Safe to clean, Your call and Managed by Windows, with the largest first. At the bottom, Disk shows the drive's free space.

Moving around

  • Click a folder to zoom into it; it grows to fill the map.
  • Click a file, or right-click anything, to show it in File Explorer.
  • Breadcrumbs in the header jump back to any parent.
  • Back and Forward work like a browser: every folder you open goes into a history.

Depth

Depth sets how many levels of folders are drawn inside each other, from 1 to 6. It starts at 3. Use the − and + buttons in the header, or [ and ]. Lower depth gives bigger, simpler tiles; higher depth shows more at once.

Rescan and theme

Rescan scans the drive again and returns you to the folder you were in, which is how you see the map after cleaning. The theme button switches between System, Light and Dark.
